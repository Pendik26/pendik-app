# Histology — High-Yield Summary

## Epithelial Tissue

- **Classified by layers**: simple (one layer) vs. stratified (multiple layers),
  and by surface cell **shape**: squamous, cuboidal, or columnar.
- **Simple squamous**: lines vessels (endothelium) and body cavities
  (mesothelium) — built for diffusion/filtration.
- **Pseudostratified ciliated columnar**: looks layered but every cell touches
  the basement membrane; lines the respiratory tract.
- **Transitional (urothelium)**: lines the urinary tract; surface cells flatten
  as the bladder distends.
- **Stratified squamous**: keratinized in skin (barrier), non-keratinized in the
  esophagus/oral cavity/vagina (abrasion resistance).

## Connective Tissue

- **Fibroblasts** produce the extracellular matrix (collagen, elastin, ground
  substance) — the most common resident cell.
- **Mast cells** store histamine/heparin in metachromatic granules and drive
  allergic/inflammatory responses.
- **Macrophages** phagocytose debris and pathogens and present antigen.
- **Cartilage**: hyaline (articular surfaces, trachea), elastic (ear,
  epiglottis), fibrocartilage (discs, menisci — avascular, no perichondrium).
- **Bone**: osteoblasts secrete osteoid → become osteocytes once mineralized
  matrix surrounds them; osteoclasts (multinucleated) resorb bone.

## Blood Cells

- **Erythrocytes**: anucleate, biconcave, ~7.5 µm — carry oxygen via hemoglobin.
- **Platelets**: anucleate cytoplasmic fragments of megakaryocytes, involved in
  clotting.
- **Granulocytes**: neutrophils (multilobed nucleus, most abundant, acute
  infection), eosinophils (bilobed, eosinophilic granules, parasites/allergy),
  basophils (rare, coarse basophilic granules, allergic response).
- **Agranulocytes**: lymphocytes (large nucleus, thin cytoplasmic rim, adaptive
  immunity), monocytes (kidney-shaped nucleus, become tissue macrophages).

## Muscle Tissue

- **Skeletal muscle**: striated, long multinucleated fibers, peripheral nuclei,
  voluntary control.
- **Cardiac muscle**: striated, branching fibers, one or two central nuclei,
  intercalated discs couple adjacent cells.
- **Smooth muscle**: non-striated, spindle-shaped cells with a single central
  nucleus, involuntary control (viscera, vessels).

## Nervous & Lymphatic Tissue

- **Neurons**: large cell body (soma) with a prominent nucleolus, dendrites, and
  a single axon; Nissl bodies (rough ER) in the cytoplasm.
- **Lymph node**: cortex (B-cell follicles with germinal centers) surrounds the
  medulla (plasma cells, macrophages); paracortex holds T cells.
- **Spleen**: white pulp (lymphoid tissue around a central arteriole — immune
  surveillance) vs. red pulp (sinusoids — filters and recycles old
  erythrocytes).

## Glands & Cell Junctions

- **Secretion modes**: merocrine (exocytosis, cell intact — eccrine sweat
  glands), apocrine (pinches off apical cytoplasm — mammary glands), holocrine
  (whole cell ruptures — sebaceous glands).
- **Exocrine vs. endocrine**: exocrine glands keep a duct to an epithelial
  surface; endocrine glands lose the duct and secrete hormones straight into
  surrounding capillaries.
- **Cell junctions**: tight junctions seal the paracellular space; desmosomes
  anchor intermediate filaments between cells (mechanical strength); gap
  junctions allow direct ion/small-molecule passage (electrical coupling);
  hemidesmosomes anchor cells to the basement membrane.
- **Kupffer cells**: liver-resident macrophages lining the sinusoids, part of
  the reticuloendothelial system.
