# 3D anatomy

**3D anatomy** (in the sidebar under Read) is a model of the whole body that
you can turn, take apart and click on. It has the skeleton, joints and
ligaments, muscles, heart and vessels, nerves, organs, the lymphatic system,
skin and body regions: about 1,800 named structures.

## The workspace

The atlas is laid out like the knowledge map: a ribbon of buttons on the
left, the **body systems** beside it, the model in the middle, and the open
structure on the right as a note. The ribbon's buttons show or hide the body
systems, find a structure, bring back the whole body, turn around the part you
click, show everything you hid, and open a random structure.

## Moving around

The model moves like the knowledge map's 3D view, and like the viewport of a
3D editor such as Blender:

| Do this | To |
| --- | --- |
| Drag | Orbit around the point you look at |
| Right-drag, middle-drag or <kbd>Shift</kbd> + drag | Pan |
| Scroll | Zoom toward the pointer |
| <kbd>W</kbd> <kbd>A</kbd> <kbd>S</kbd> <kbd>D</kbd> | Fly forward, left, back, right |
| <kbd>Q</kbd> <kbd>E</kbd> | Fly down, up (<kbd>Shift</kbd>: faster) |
| Arrow keys | Orbit (<kbd>Shift</kbd>: slide the view) |

On a touch screen, drag to turn the body, drag with two fingers to move it,
and pinch to zoom.

- The **axis gizmo** at the top left of the model shows which way the body
  faces, named as anatomists name the directions: **Anterior** and
  **Posterior** (blue), **Superior** and **Inferior** (green), **Sinistra**
  and **Dextra** (red). Click any of them to look from that side.
- The buttons under the model do the same: Anterior, Posterior, Sinistra,
  Dextra, Superior and Inferior.
- **Persp** and **Ortho** at the top switch between perspective and
  orthographic drawing. Orthographic keeps sizes true at any depth, as in an
  atlas drawing.
- **Auto-rotate** turns the body slowly; **Grid** shows the floor, with the
  lateral axis in red and the sagittal axis in blue; **Controls** lists the
  controls.
- The buttons at the bottom right turn the body left and right, zoom in and
  out, and bring the whole body back into view.
- **Turn around the part you click** (on the ribbon, on at first): the view
  slides to the part you click, so turning the body turns it around that
  part.
- The tab at the top says which side of the body faces you: anterior,
  posterior, lateralis sinistra or dextra, superior or inferior.
- The button at the top right shows the atlas **full screen**, with Alfond
  still there. <kbd>Esc</kbd> or the same button leaves it.

## Body systems

The **body systems** panel switches each system on or off. The page opens
with the skeleton only. On a narrow screen, open it from the ribbon.

- Each system downloads the first time you switch it on. Its size is shown
  under it; the muscles are the biggest at about 4 MB. After that it is
  saved on your device, and it works offline.
- The **Opacity** slider under a system makes it see-through, so you can see
  what lies underneath. Skin starts half see-through.
- The chips under a system switch its parts on and off, such as the muscles
  of the upper limb or the central nervous system. The fasciae start hidden,
  because they would cover every muscle.
- **Muscle attachments** shows on the bones where each muscle starts (its
  origin, in red) and ends (its insertion, in blue).

## Naming a structure

**Hover** over a part to see its name in English and Latin, and **click** it
to open it as a note on the right. The selected part glows, gets an
outline, and shows through in green where other structures cover it, so a
nerve under a muscle stays visible. **Double-click** it to fly to it.

- **Click the same place again** to select the structure beneath, then the
  one beneath that: skin, muscle, bone. The note lists everything at that
  spot, front to back, under **At this spot**; click any of them.
- **Ctrl** (⌘ on a Mac) **+ click** adds a structure to the selection, or
  takes it out. With several selected, **Only these** shows just them and
  **Hide these** hides them all. <kbd>Esc</kbd> clears the extra ones first.

You can also type a name into **Find a structure** (at the top of the body
systems), in English or Latin: "femur" or "os femoris", "deltoid", "vagus",
or a group such as "cranial nerves". <kbd>Enter</kbd> opens the first match,
and the arrow keys move through the list.

On a phone, tap a part. Its name shows at the top of the model; tap the name
to jump to the note about it.

The note for a structure shows:

- its English name, and under it the Latin name with its side, as in
  *ren dexter* or *os femoris dextrum*. For organs whose clinical words come
  from Greek, the Greek root follows: the kidney shows *Gr. nephros*
  (nephritis, nephrology), the lung *pneumon*, a joint *arthron*;
- **Ask Alfond**, which asks Alfond to explain the structure, briefly and for
  the exam;
- **What it is**: a short description, when there is one;
- its properties: the system and side as tags (*#dextra*, *#sinistra*), the
  Latin name, the Greek root, and the group it belongs to ("Bones of lower
  limb › Bones of free part of lower limb"). Click a group to list
  everything in it;
- its **landmarks**: the named points on a bone, such as the greater
  trochanter, with their Latin names. Click one to show where it is;
- **Find in your notes**, which searches the flashcards, quizzes and chapters
  for its name.

The arrows at the top of the note go **back** and **forward** through the
structures you have opened. When nothing is open, the note suggests where to
start and lists the ones you opened recently.

Under the properties are these buttons:

| Button | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Focus** | Zooms in on the structure |
| **Only this** | Hides everything else, until you press **Show the rest** |
| **Hide** | Hides the structure. **Show all** under the model brings back everything you hid |
| **Sinistra** / **Dextra** | Opens the same structure on the other side |
| **Where it attaches** | For a muscle: shows the skeleton with the muscle's origin and insertion on it, and makes the other muscles see-through |

## Keys

| Key | Does |
| --- | --- |
| <kbd>/</kbd> | Find a structure |
| <kbd>W</kbd> <kbd>A</kbd> <kbd>S</kbd> <kbd>D</kbd> <kbd>Q</kbd> <kbd>E</kbd> | Fly (<kbd>Shift</kbd>: faster) |
| <kbd>1</kbd> <kbd>3</kbd> <kbd>7</kbd> | Anterior, dextra and superior views (with <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>: posterior, sinistra, inferior) |
| <kbd>9</kbd> | Look from the opposite side |
| <kbd>5</kbd> | Perspective or orthographic |
| <kbd>R</kbd> | Auto-rotate |
| <kbd>G</kbd> | Floor grid |
| <kbd>F</kbd> | Focus on the open structure |
| <kbd>H</kbd> | Hide it (<kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>H</kbd>: show everything you hid) |
| <kbd>O</kbd> | Only this (again to show the rest) |
| <kbd>0</kbd> | Whole body |
| Arrow keys | Orbit (<kbd>Shift</kbd>: slide the view) |
| <kbd>+</kbd> <kbd>−</kbd> | Zoom in or out |
| <kbd>Backspace</kbd> | Back to the structure you opened before (<kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>Backspace</kbd>: forward) |
| <kbd>Esc</kbd> | Close the open structure |

## Sharing

The address in your browser changes as you choose. Copy it to share or
bookmark the same structure with the same systems on.

## Where it comes from

The models and descriptions are from [Z-Anatomy](https://www.z-anatomy.com/),
an open atlas built on BodyParts3D. The descriptions follow Wikipedia. They
are shared under a Creative Commons licence (CC BY-SA), and the atlas may not
be used commercially. It shows gross anatomy only, with no histology or
embryology. Always check details against your lectures.
