# Chapter 4: Dermis, Hypodermis & Sensory Endings

Below the epidermis lies the **dermis**, the connective tissue that gives
skin its strength and elasticity, carries its blood vessels and nerves,
and houses the roots of its appendages. Below that is the **hypodermis**,
a cushion of fat that joins the skin to the structures underneath. This
chapter covers both, and then the sensory nerve endings scattered through
them: the reason the skin is the body's largest sense organ.

## The dermis

The dermis has two layers that differ in thickness and in the kind of
connective tissue they are made of.

### Stratum papillare (papillary layer)

- A **thinner** layer.
- Made of **loose connective tissue**.
- Contains:
  - **fibroblasts, macrophages and mast cells**;
  - the **dermal papillae**, which **interdigitate with the epidermal
    ridges** (Chapter 3);
  - **blood vessels** (capillary loops) that **nourish the avascular
    epidermis by diffusion**;
  - **anchoring fibrils** of **type VII collagen**, which tie the
    epidermal basal lamina to the dermis;
  - **Meissner's corpuscles** (touch receptors) in the papillae.

### Stratum reticulare (reticular layer)

- A much **thicker** layer, making up most of the dermis.
- Made of **dense irregular connective tissue**, mainly:
  - **collagen fibers (type I)** in thick, interwoven bundles, which give
    the skin its tensile strength;
  - **elastic fibers**, which let it recoil after stretching.
- Hair follicles, sweat and sebaceous glands, and larger vessels and
  nerves lie here.

| | Papillary layer | Reticular layer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Thickness | Thin | Thick (most of the dermis) |
| Connective tissue | **Loose** | **Dense irregular** |
| Fibers | Fine collagen (types I and III), type VII anchoring fibrils | Coarse **type I** collagen, elastic fibers |
| Contents | Capillary loops, Meissner's corpuscles, immune cells | Appendages, larger vessels, Ruffini endings |

With age and sun exposure, collagen and elastic fibers in the dermis
degrade, which is why skin wrinkles and loses its spring. The main
directions of the collagen bundles form **Langer's lines**; surgical
incisions made along them gape less and heal with thinner scars.

## The hypodermis

  <img src="/histology/1.2/i-image48.jpg"
    alt="Low-power section of skin with a long blue arrow spanning the deep
    fatty layer beneath the dermis" loading="lazy" width="760" height="511" />
  <figcaption>The hypodermis (blue arrow): the deep layer of loose connective
    tissue and fat beneath the dermis. <span class="figure-source">Histology
    practicum quiz</span></figcaption>
</figure>

The hypodermis (**subcutis**, *hipodermis*) is:

- made of **loose connective tissue**;
- rich in **adipose cells (adipocytes)**, grouped into lobules;
- **extensively vascularized**;
- the home of **Pacinian corpuscles**, sensory endings that detect
  **pressure**.

It insulates the body, stores energy, cushions against blows and lets the
skin slide over the structures beneath it. Strictly speaking it is not
part of the skin (it corresponds to the superficial fascia of anatomy),
but it is studied with it. Its thickness varies greatly between sites and
people; the layer of fat in it is called the *panniculus adiposus*.

## Sensory nerve endings

  <figcaption>The sensory endings of the skin and where they sit: free nerve
    endings and Merkel discs in the epidermis, Meissner's corpuscles in the
    dermal papillae, Ruffini and Krause endings in the dermis, and Pacinian

    module</span></figcaption>
</figure>

The skin's nerve endings are either **free** (bare branches of the axon)
or attached to a special structure: a Merkel cell, or a **capsule** of
connective tissue and flattened cells. The capsule shapes what the ending
responds to.

### Corpusculum Merkeli (Merkel's disc)

- **Unencapsulated.**
- **Free nerve endings attached to Merkel cells.**
- Located in the **basal layer of the epidermis**.
- A receptor for **light touch / tactile sensation**, particularly
  sustained touch and fine texture (numerous on the fingertips).

### Corpusculum Vater-Pacini (Pacinian corpuscle)

<figure class="diagram slide-figure">
  <img src="/ebook-figures/histology-1.2/pacini-drawing.webp"
    alt="Drawing of an oval Pacinian corpuscle made of many concentric layers
    around a central nerve ending" loading="lazy" width="627" height="1000" />
  <figcaption>A Pacinian corpuscle: many concentric lamellae around a central
    nerve ending, like a cut onion. <span class="figure-source">Page 31,
    Integumentary System module</span></figcaption>
</figure>

- **Oval, onion-shaped.**
- A nerve ending surrounded by a **capsule of concentric layers**
  (lamellae), made of **collagen fibers and flattened, endothelial-like
  cells**, with fluid between them.
- Located in the **deep dermis and the subcutaneous tissue**.
- A receptor for **crude touch, sustained pressure and vibration**.
- Large (up to 1–2 mm), so it is easy to spot even at low power.

### Corpusculum Meissner (Meissner's corpuscle)

<figure class="diagram slide-figure">
  <img src="/ebook-figures/histology-1.2/meissner-drawing.webp"
    alt="Drawing of an elongated Meissner&#x27;s corpuscle with a branched nerve
    ending inside" loading="lazy" width="581" height="1000" />
  <figcaption>A Meissner's corpuscle: a cylindrical capsule with a branched
    nerve ending winding through it. <span class="figure-source">Page 32,
    Integumentary System module</span></figcaption>
</figure>

- **Cylindrical** (elongated, oval).
- **Branched, encapsulated** nerve endings.
- A **thin capsule of connective tissue**, with **flattened cells
  arranged transversely** inside it (like a stack of coins).
- Located **in the dermal papillae**, just beneath the epidermis.
- A receptor for **light touch**; abundant on the lips, fingertips,
  palms and soles.

### Corpusculum Ruffini (Ruffini ending)

<figure class="diagram slide-figure">
  <img src="/ebook-figures/histology-1.2/ruffini-drawing.webp"
    alt="Drawing of a small spindle-shaped Ruffini ending with spray-like nerve
    branches" loading="lazy" width="647" height="1000" />
  <figcaption>A Ruffini ending: a thin capsule enclosing nerve branches spread
    like a spray. <span class="figure-source">Page 33, Integumentary System
    module</span></figcaption>
</figure>

- **Cylindrical, and smaller than Meissner's corpuscles.**
- Nerve endings that **branch in a spray-like pattern**, encapsulated.
- A **thin capsule** of connective tissue.
- Located **in the dermis**.
- A receptor for **pressure** (and for stretch of the skin, which signals
  joint and finger position).

### Corpusculum Krause (Krause end bulb)

<figure class="diagram slide-figure">
  <img src="/ebook-figures/histology-1.2/krause-drawing.webp"
    alt="Drawing of a round Krause end bulb with a coiled branched nerve ending
    inside a capsule" loading="lazy" width="801" height="1000" />
  <figcaption>A Krause end bulb: a round capsule with a coiled, branched nerve
    ending inside. <span class="figure-source">Page 34, Integumentary System
    module</span></figcaption>
</figure>

- **Round / spherical.**
- **Branched, encapsulated** nerve endings.
- A **thick capsule** of connective tissue.
- Located **in the dermis** (also in mucous membranes such as the lips and
  conjunctiva).
- The practicum lists it as a receptor for **pressure**. Older textbooks
  called it a **cold** receptor; its exact role is still debated, so give
  the practicum's answer in the practicum.

### Free nerve endings

The simplest endings: bare branches of sensory axons reaching into the
epidermis. They detect **pain, temperature and itch**, and they are by far
the most numerous. A related type wraps around the base of each hair
follicle (the **root hair plexus**), sensing hair movement.

## Summary: the five named endings

| Ending | Shape | Capsule | Location | Senses |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Merkel** | Disc on a Merkel cell | **None** | Basal epidermis | Light touch |
| **Meissner** | **Cylindrical** | Thin; transverse flattened cells | **Dermal papillae** | **Light touch** |
| **Vater-Pacini** | **Oval, onion-like** | **Concentric lamellae** | Deep dermis, **hypodermis** | Crude touch, **pressure, vibration** |
| **Ruffini** | Cylindrical, small | Thin; spray-like branches | Dermis | Pressure, stretch |
| **Krause** | **Round** | **Thick** | Dermis | Pressure (practicum) |

Two contrasts are asked most often:

- **Meissner vs Pacini**: Meissner is small, cylindrical and high up in
  the papillae (light touch); Pacini is large, onion-like and deep
  (pressure and vibration).
- **Meissner vs Ruffini vs Krause**: all encapsulated and branched.
  Meissner is in the papillae; Ruffini is small with a spray of branches;
  Krause is round with a thick capsule.
