# Chapter 6: Practicum Guide: Identifying the Slides

The histology practicum for this block is an identification exam: you are
shown a slide or a micrograph, often with an arrow or a label, and asked
to name the tissue, a layer, a cell or a structure, sometimes followed by
a question about its function. This chapter turns Chapters 1–5 into a
routine you can run at the microscope, slide by slide.

## A routine for any slide

1. **Scan at low power** (4× or 10×) before anything else. Is it a sheet
   of one tissue, or an organ with layers? Is there a surface with
   epithelium on it?
2. **If there is a keratinized surface on top, it is skin.** Go to the
   skin routine below.
3. **If the whole field is pink fibers or cells, it is probably
   muscle.** Decide the plane of section, then use the muscle routine.
4. **Only then zoom in** to answer the arrow: a layer, a cell or a
   structure.
5. For a "function" follow-up question, name the job in two or three
   words: "light touch", "holocrine, sebum", "pressure and vibration".

## Muscle routine

**Step 1: plane of section.** Long strips = longitudinal (*membujur*);
a mosaic of round or polygonal profiles = transverse (*melintang*). In the
atlas figure, **OL = transverse** and **IC = longitudinal**.

**Step 2: striations and nuclei.**

| You see | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| No striations, spindle cells, **one central elongated nucleus** | **Smooth muscle** |
| Striations, **many nuclei at the periphery** under the sarcolemma | **Skeletal muscle** |
| Striations, **one central nucleus**, **branching**, **intercalated discs** | **Cardiac muscle** |

**Step 3: the follow-up questions**, and their answers:

- Shape of smooth muscle cells? **Fusiform (spindle).**
- Position of the smooth muscle nucleus? **Central.**
- Innervation of smooth muscle? **Autonomic nervous system** (the junction
  is called a *synapse*).
- Position of skeletal muscle nuclei? **Peripheral, beneath the
  sarcolemma.**
- Neurotransmitter for skeletal muscle? **Acetylcholine**, at the **motor
  end plate / neuromuscular junction**.
- Island-like bundles of myofibrils in a cross-cut skeletal fiber?
  **Cohnheim's areas.**
- Connective tissue around one fiber? **Endomysium.** Around a fascicle?
  **Perimysium.** Around the muscle? **Epimysium.**
- Dark band? **A band (anisotropic).** Light band? **I band
  (isotropic).**
- Dark line in the middle of the I band? **Z line.** In the middle of
  the A band? **M line.** Light zone in the middle of the A band? **H
  zone.**
- Unit between two Z lines? **Sarcomere.**
- Dark irregular lines crossing cardiac fibers? **Intercalated discs.**
- Fibers that branch and rejoin in cardiac muscle? **Anastomosis.**

**Traps**

- **Cardiac vs skeletal**: both striated. Nuclei central + branches +
  discs = cardiac.
- **Smooth vs cardiac** in cross-section: both have central nuclei. Look
  for striations or discs in any longitudinal part of the field.
- **Smooth muscle vs tendon or nerve**: in tendon the flat nuclei sit
  between collagen bundles; in nerve the fibers are wavy and paler, with
  myelin spaces.

## Skin routine

**Step 1: thick or thin?**

- **Stratum lucidum present**, very thick stratum corneum, no hair
  follicles → **thick skin** (palms, soles).
- No lucidum, thin corneum, hair follicles and sebaceous glands →
  **thin skin**.

**Step 2: which layer is the arrow on?** Count from the surface down:

| Layer | Clue |
| --- | --- |
| **Stratum corneum** | Pink, flaky, no nuclei |
| **Stratum lucidum** | Thin, glassy, pale band; thick skin only |
| **Stratum granulosum** | Flattened cells with dark granules |
| **Stratum spinosum** | Many layers of polygonal cells with "spines" |
| **Stratum basale** | One row of dark cuboidal/columnar cells on the basement membrane |
| **Papillary dermis** | Thin loose connective tissue in the papillae |
| **Reticular dermis** | Thick dense irregular connective tissue |
| **Hypodermis** | Fat and loose connective tissue beneath the dermis |

**Step 3: the follow-up questions**, and their answers:

- Type of epithelium of the epidermis? **Keratinized stratified squamous
  epithelium.**
- How is the epidermis nourished? **By diffusion from the dermis** (it
  has no blood vessels).
- Layer found only in thick skin? **Stratum lucidum**, whose cells contain
  **eleidin**.
- Spines on spinosum cells? **Desmosomes (intercellular bridges)**; the
  fine strands running into them are **tonofibrils**.
- Pale cell among the basal cells? **Melanocyte** (makes **eumelanin**).
- Dendritic cell in the spinosum, no melanin, shown with special stains?
  **Langerhans cell** (immune, macrophage-like).
- Layer of dermis just under the epidermis? **Stratum papillare** (loose
  connective tissue).
- Deep fatty layer? **Hypodermis (subcutis).**

## Nerve endings

| You see | Answer | Senses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Large oval "onion" of concentric lamellae, deep dermis or hypodermis | **Vater-Pacini corpuscle** | **Pressure and vibration** |
| Small cylindrical capsule filling a **dermal papilla**, transverse stacked cells | **Meissner's corpuscle** | **Light touch** |
| Unencapsulated nerve ending on a basal cell | **Merkel's disc** | Light touch |
| Small cylinder with spray-like branches, dermis | Ruffini ending | Pressure |
| Round, thick capsule, dermis | Krause end bulb | Pressure (practicum) |

## Appendages

| You see | Answer | Follow-up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Circle of rings around a central hair | **Hair follicle, transverse section** | Hair layers inside out: medulla, cortex, cuticle |
| Clusters of large, pale, foamy cells next to a follicle | **Sebaceous gland** | **Holocrine**; opens into the follicle |
| Many small round tubules in the deep dermis, pale secretory and dark ducts | **Eccrine sweat gland** | **Merocrine**; opens onto the skin surface |
| Large tubules with **wide lumens** (axilla) | **Apocrine sweat gland** | Opens into a hair follicle |
| Flat cell between secretory cells and basal lamina | **Myoepithelial cell** | In the secretory portion only |
| Keratin plate on the back of a fingertip | **Nail** | Eponychium proximal, hyponychium distal |

## Last-minute checklist

- **Striations?** Skeletal or cardiac. **Nuclei peripheral?** Skeletal.
  **Discs and branches?** Cardiac. **Neither striations nor discs?**
  Smooth.
- **OL = transverse, IC = longitudinal.**
- Epi- (whole muscle), peri- (fascicle), endo- (one fiber).
- A band dark, I band light, Z in I, M and H in A, Z to Z = sarcomere.
- **Lucidum = thick skin.** Eleidin in the lucidum, keratohyalin in the
  granulosum.
- Melanocyte (basale, pigment), Langerhans (spinosum, immune), Merkel
  (basale, touch).
- Papillary = loose, reticular = dense irregular.
- Meissner = papillae, light touch; Pacini = onion, pressure and
  vibration.
- Sebaceous = holocrine into the follicle; eccrine = merocrine onto the
  surface; apocrine = wide lumen into the follicle.
