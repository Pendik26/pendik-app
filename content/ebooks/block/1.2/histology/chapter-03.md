# Chapter 3: Skin Structure & the Epidermis

The skin (integument) is the body's largest organ: about 15% of body
weight and close to 2 m² in an adult. It keeps water in and microbes out,
blocks ultraviolet light, helps control body temperature, makes vitamin D,
and is packed with sensory endings. The integumentary system is the skin
plus its **appendages**: hair, nails and glands (Chapter 5).

This chapter covers the overall layout of the skin and its outer layer,
the **epidermis**: its strata, the difference between thick and thin
skin, and the four kinds of cells that live in it besides keratinocytes.

The skin has two layers, and it sits on a third:

| Layer | Tissue | Origin |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Epidermis** | Keratinized stratified squamous epithelium | Surface **ectoderm** |
| **Dermis** | Connective tissue: a thin loose papillary layer over a thick dense reticular layer | **Mesoderm** |
| **Hypodermis** (subcutis) | Loose connective tissue rich in fat | Mesoderm; not strictly part of the skin |

The boundary between epidermis and dermis is not flat. **Epidermal
ridges** (rete ridges) project down into the dermis and interlock with
**dermal papillae** projecting up, like the teeth of a zipper. This
increases the area of contact, anchors the epidermis against shearing
forces, and brings capillaries close to the epidermis, which has **no
blood vessels of its own**: it is nourished by diffusion from the dermis.
On the fingertips the ridges form the fingerprint pattern.

## The epidermis

The epidermis is **keratinized stratified squamous epithelium**. Its main
cell is the **keratinocyte**, which is born in the deepest layer and
pushed toward the surface, changing as it goes: it fills with keratin,
loses its nucleus and organelles, and dies as a flat, tough scale that is
finally shed. That whole journey (**keratinization**) takes about two to
four weeks.

**1. Stratum basale** (stratum germinativum)

- A **single layer of cuboidal or columnar, basophilic cells** sitting on
  the basal lamina, to which they are attached by **hemidesmosomes**.
- Joined to each other on their apical and lateral surfaces by
  **desmosomes**.
- **High mitotic activity**: it contains the **progenitor (stem) cells**
  that give rise to all keratinocytes.
- Melanocytes and Merkel cells sit among the basal cells.

**2. Stratum spinosum**

    spiny bridges, with yellow arrows" loading="lazy" width="760" height="511"
    />
  <figcaption>The stratum spinosum (the boxed area, enlarged). The cells are
    polygonal and appear to bristle with short spines where they are joined to
    their neighbors; the yellow arrows point to tonofibrils. <span
    class="figure-source">Page 10, Integumentary System
    module</span></figcaption>
</figure>

- Several layers of **polygonal cells** with central nuclei.
- In routine sections the cells shrink apart but stay joined at their
  **desmosomes**, so each cell appears covered with short "spines", the
  **intercellular bridges** that give the layer its name.
- Bundles of keratin intermediate filaments, **tonofibrils**, run into
  the desmosomes and are visible as fine cytoplasmic strands.
- **Langerhans cells** are found mainly here.
- Together, the basale and spinosum are called the **stratum Malpighii**,
  the living, dividing part of the epidermis.

**3. Stratum granulosum**

- Three to five layers of **flattened cells** full of dark, basophilic
  **keratohyalin granules**.
- The cells also secrete **lamellar granules** of lipid into the spaces
  between cells, which make the skin waterproof.
- Above this layer the cells die: nuclei and organelles break down.

**4. Stratum lucidum**

- Several layers of **flattened, pale cells**.
- **Anucleate** (no nuclei).
- The cytoplasm contains **eleidin**, a transformation product of
  keratohyalin, which makes the layer look clear and glassy (*lucidum*,
  "bright").
- **Found only in thick skin.**

**5. Stratum corneum**

- **15–20 layers of flattened, dead, anucleate cells** (corneocytes).
- The cytoplasm is filled with **refractile filamentous keratin**.
- The superficial cells are continually shed (**desquamation**) as the
  last step of keratinization.

## Thick skin and thin skin

<figure class="diagram slide-figure">
  <img src="/ebook-figures/histology-1.2/thin-vs-thick.webp"
    alt="Micrograph and drawing comparing thick skin with its lucidum to the
    epidermal layers" loading="lazy" width="1000" height="463" />
  <figcaption>Thick skin: a micrograph and a drawing of its strata, including
    the stratum lucidum between the granulosum and the corneum. <span
    class="figure-source">Page 7, Integumentary System
    module</span></figcaption>
</figure>

"Thick" and "thin" describe the **epidermis**, not the whole skin.

| | Thin skin | Thick skin |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Where** | Most of the body | **Palms and soles** |
| **Stratum corneum** | Thin | Very thick |
| **Stratum lucidum** | Absent | **Present** |
| **Hair and sebaceous glands** | Present | Absent |
| **Sweat glands** | Present | Very numerous |
| **Epidermal ridges** | Low | Deep (fingerprints) |

<figure class="diagram slide-figure">
  <img src="/histology/1.2/i-image26.jpg"
    alt="Thick skin with a very thick, pale stratum corneum over deep epidermal
    ridges" loading="lazy" width="760" height="508" />
  <figcaption>Thick skin: an enormously thick stratum corneum and, just beneath
    it, the pale stratum lucidum. If you see a lucidum, it is thick skin. <span
    class="figure-source">Histology practicum quiz</span></figcaption>
</figure>

The quick rule at the microscope: **a stratum lucidum or a stratum corneum
thicker than the rest of the epidermis means thick skin**; hair follicles
in the section mean thin skin.

  <figcaption>The cell types of the epidermis and where they live: keratinocytes

Keratinocytes make up about 90% of the epidermis. The others:

### Melanocytes

- A **round cell body with long cytoplasmic processes** (dendrites).
- Located **between the cells of the stratum basale**.
- They **produce the pigment eumelanin** in organelles called
  melanosomes and pass it through their processes to the surrounding
  keratinocytes, where it forms a cap over the nucleus that shields the
  DNA from ultraviolet light.
- Skin color depends on **how much melanin is made and transferred, not
  on the number of melanocytes**, which is similar in light and dark
  skin.
- In H&E they appear as **pale "clear" cells in the basal layer**.

### Langerhans cells

- **Morphologically similar to melanocytes**, with long dendritic
  processes, **but they do not produce melanin**.
- Found **mainly in the stratum spinosum**.
- **Immune cells**: they act like **macrophages**, capturing antigens
  that cross the skin and carrying them to lymph nodes to present to T
  cells.
- In H&E they are hard to see; special stains (gold chloride, or
  immunostaining for CD1a) show them as dark cells with branching
  processes.

### Merkel cells

- **Morphologically similar to keratinocytes**, but their cytoplasm
  contains **small, dense granules** (neurosecretory granules).
- Found in the **basal layer**, especially in **thick skin** (fingertips).
- A **free nerve ending** expands into a disc at the base of each cell;
  together they form the **Merkel corpuscle** (Merkel's disc), a
  **mechanoreceptor** for light, sustained touch (Chapter 4).

### Lymphocytes

- **Round cells with a round nucleus** of coarse, densely packed
  chromatin.
- Most are **immature T lymphocytes**, whose maturation in the epidermis
  is supported by keratinocytes.
- With Langerhans cells and keratinocytes, they form the skin's immune
  system, **SALT (skin-associated lymphoid tissue)**.

| Cell | Where | What it does | How to recognize it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Keratinocyte | All layers | Keratin, barrier | The ordinary cells of every stratum |
| **Melanocyte** | Stratum basale | Eumelanin | Pale cell among basal cells, dendrites |
| **Langerhans cell** | Stratum spinosum | Antigen presentation | Dendritic, no melanin; special stains |
| **Merkel cell** | Stratum basale | Touch receptor (with its nerve ending) | Dense granules; thick skin |
| Lymphocyte | Epidermis | Immunity (SALT) | Small round cell, dense nucleus |
